### 1.5 Frost and cold events

#### Expected annual summary

- Freeze days per year (≤32°F): **~9** (average) / up to ~20 in a bad year
- Frost days per year (≤36°F): **~25**
- Frost window: **late November – late February**
- Coldest month: January
- Record low: 18°F (January 21, 1985)
- USDA Hardiness Zone: 9b (25–30°F)

#### Expected timing

First fall frost (first night ≤36°F):

- Earliest to expect (10%): **November 18**
- Typical (50%): **December 14**
- Latest (90%): **January 16**

Last spring frost (last night ≤36°F):

- Earliest end (10%): **January 18**
- Typical (50%): **February 17**
- Safe for tender crops (90%): **March 16**

Monthly frost/freeze distribution (typical year):

- November: 0–3 frost days, 0–1 freeze
- December: 3–9 frost days, 1–6 freeze
- January: 11–12 frost days, 2–10 freeze (peak month)
- February: 1–12 frost days, 0–9 freeze

#### Actual recorded data — last 2 winters

Data source: Weather Underground, KBKV station (Brooksville).

**Winter 2024–2025 (normal year):**

- Frost days: ~25 — Freeze days: ~9
- First frost: Nov 23 (31°F) — Last frost: Feb 21 (35°F)
- Coldest low: 23°F (Jan 25)
- Nov: 1 freeze, 2 frost — Dec: 6 freeze (low 14°F on Dec 26), 3 frost — Jan: 2 freeze (low 23°F), 10 frost — Feb: 0 freeze, 1 frost

**Winter 2025–2026 (extreme — USDA disaster):**

- Frost days: ~26 — Freeze days: ~20 (2× average)
- First frost: Dec 12 (33°F) — Last freeze: Feb 25 (27°F)
- Coldest low: 17°F (Feb 2) — near all-time record
- Dec: 1 freeze (low 24°F), 2 frost — Jan: 10 freeze (low 23°F), 1 frost — Feb: 9 freeze (low 17°F), 3 frost

#### Historical freeze events

- **1894–95 (Great Freeze):** Back-to-back freezes destroyed citrus across Northern Florida
- **1934 (December):** 20°F in Brooksville; 14 million boxes of citrus lost statewide
- **1957–58 (Winter):** 22°F in Brooksville; severe citrus crop damage
- **2026 (January–February):** 17°F at KBKV; USDA designated Hernando County a disaster area; $3.17B in statewide agricultural losses

#### Notes and definitions

**Frost vs. freeze vs. hard freeze:**

- **Frost day (33–36°F air temp):** Ice crystals form on plant surfaces due to radiative cooling, even though air is above 32°F. Damages tender foliage and flowers; most established trees survive.
- **Light freeze (29–32°F):** Air reaches freezing. Water inside plant cells can freeze. Kills tender vegetation; citrus fruit can be damaged.
- **Hard freeze (≤28°F):** Can kill citrus, damage mature trees, destroy crops.

**About the probability percentages:** These are derived from decades of historical records. They are not predictions for any single year — they represent the distribution across many years. "90%" means in 9 out of 10 past years, frost had passed (spring) or arrived (fall) by that date.

**For planting decisions:** Use the 90% dates (safe after March 16 in spring, expect frost by Nov 18 in fall) for frost-sensitive crops. Recent winters show the median dates can be significantly exceeded. Plan for occasional lows in the high teens (17–23°F).
